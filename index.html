THE STORY

He woke to the sound of a number.

Not a clock, not a voice — a number, hanging in the air above his chest like a coin held up to the light. 0. It pulsed once, faintly, the way a heartbeat looks from the outside, and then it was gone.

The ceiling above him was wrong. Too high, too dark, made of stone that had never seen a lamp. The air smelled of rain and iron and something sweet underneath, like fruit left too long in the sun. He sat up fast, and the world tilted, and when it settled he was still somewhere else.

His hands were his hands. His clothes were not. Rough linen, patched at the elbow, the kind of thing a farmer’s son might wear to a funeral. There was no phone in his pocket. There was no pocket.

‘Hello?’ His voice came out smaller than he expected.

The room answered with silence, and then — not silence. A whisper, thin as thread, coming from everywhere and nowhere. It said one word.

‘Priceless.’

He didn’t know what that meant. He would learn. By the end of the day he would learn that in this world, everything had a price — the bread on the table, the roof over his head, the blood in his veins. Everything except him. And that was the problem. That was the whole problem.

Because a thing with no price is a thing nobody can buy. And a thing nobody can buy is a thing everybody wants to own.
